- 2026-10-19 — build-3 (build room, `wt-build3`, branch `build/plan135-outlier-analysis`): **#135 —
  the condition-5 single-tick stall (max 6410 ms) is an SDK pipeline restart, not rapid movement.
  Verdict: the operator's rapid-movement hypothesis is **RULED OUT** as the cause; the stall's cause
  is identified.** Log forensics on our own recorded evidence
  (`tests/results/2026-10-19-plan112-tick-latency-baseline.raw.log`, sha256
  `5a12caf6ea7f6de4921a75442089ceb97dad70708993fb29ec1268da9670df50`; the copy in
  `wt-hw/tests/results/` is byte-identical to the one now on `main`). Every claim below cites a
  raw-log line number. The hardware record and the raw log were **not** edited — this PR adds one
  file, the note.

  **Finding 1 — the stall is a vendor-SDK pipeline restart, and it is the *only* pipeline restart
  in the run.** Immediately before the outlier the SDK tears its whole sensor pipeline down and
  rebuilds it: `L16270` `Stop imu stream error: std::bad_cast` → `L16271` `Try to stop pipeline!` →
  `L16276` `Sensor stream stopped, sensorType=OB_SENSOR_IR_RIGHT` → `L16284` `Stop pipeline done!` →
  `L16285` `Try to start streams!` → `L16291` `start OB_STREAM_IR_RIGHT stream done` → `L16293`
  `Pipeline start done!` (19:55:09.917). The other two `Stop imu stream error: std::bad_cast`
  occurrences in the run are `L73`/`L76` (device bring-up, 19:51:57) and `L25612` (teardown,
  19:57:24) — the outlier's is the only *mid-scan* one, and the only one that rebuilds the pipeline
  while the scan is live. The SDK's own frame-wait warnings bracket it exactly: `L16262`
  (19:55:04.169) and `L16462` (19:55:13.285, `43 logs in 6102ms`). The stall is **not** a
  first-time glitch: `L16269` (19:55:07.183) already shows `29 logs in 3013ms` — 9.6 logs/s, the
  densest frame-timeout burst in the entire run (the next densest is 7.05/s at `L16462`; every other
  one is ≤0.67/s).

  **Finding 2 — the frame stream actually died, and the tick was waiting on it.** Between the first
  frame-timeout warning and the restart completing (`L16262`→`L16293`, 19:55:04.169→19:55:09.917,
  5.75 s) the SDK delivered exactly **one** `FRAME_READY` line, against **482** in the 16.3 s
  immediately before (measured by line range, not by timestamp bucketing) — ~29.6 frames/s before
  versus ~0.2/s across the gap, recovering to ~28.1 frames/s after. The tick's own work was never the problem:
  in the outlier window `L16662` every sub-segment is normal — `pose p95=0.01 max=0.01`,
  `getter p95=0.00 max=0.18`, `post p95=2.80 max=2.81`, `bcast p95=0.49 max=0.76` — so the 6410 ms
  was spent *waiting* for a frame the SDK was not producing.

  On the window's own fields: `n=30` and `rate=2.6/s` (vs ~8.6/s) are that one slow period's
  arithmetic, not a compute stall. `p95=1141.74` and `max=6410.48` are the **two largest of the
  window's 30 periods** (nearest-rank p95 lands on the 29th), so the window contains **two** slow
  ticks — one ≥1.14 s and one 6.41 s — with the other 28 at or below 1141.74 ms and `p50=121.94`.
  The 6.41 s period straddles the window boundary: the flush lands *after* the blocked tick, so the
  period is recorded against `L16662` while the blocked wall-clock sits in the preceding window
  `L16294`, which is **9.4 s long** where every other window in the run is 5.0–5.1 s (it holds 27
  fast ticks, `max=123.55`, and then the block). The next window `L17127` is back to `n=41`,
  `rate=8.2/s`, `max=189.65`.

  **Finding 3 — the trigger is an oversized frame, and the motion signal is absent.** The SDK logged
  the oversized frames itself: four `:450` warnings at the stall (`L16267`, `L16268` size=2623176;
  `L16278`, `L16280` size=3065112/3093552; expected=2304000), and they are the run's **only
  mid-scan** ones — the run has six `:450` warnings in total, and the other two are at teardown.
  Note the line number: the request asked about `VideoSensor.cpp:455` (`data size does not match the
  expectation`, 28 occurrences run-wide) — **the outlier's warning is `:450`**, `data size is larger
  than expected`, a *different* message and a *different* class. There are **zero** `:455` warnings
  inside the bracket. The two `:450` warnings outside the outlier (`L25486`, `L25487`, 19:56:59) sit
  in the run's teardown, not in a stall. So the sequence is: an oversized frame → frames dropped →
  `Stop imu stream error: std::bad_cast` → pipeline restart → the tick waits ~6.4 s for frames.

  **Finding 4 — the rapid-movement hypothesis is ruled out by the run's own control.** Three
  independent checks say no:
  - *Tracking loss does not track stall size.* The outlier's LOST burst (`L16309`–`L16348`, 14
    frames) is **smaller** than the one at `L18703`–`L18808` (30 frames) and than the run's biggest
    (`L24233`–`L24359`, 32 frames) — yet it is the one carrying the 6410 ms stall. The biggest
    LOST bursts in the run (`L23717`–`L23811`, 31; `L24233`–`L24359`, 32) coincide with windows
    whose tick max is only 200–361 ms (`L24088` max=200, `L24780` max=361). Tracking loss and
    stall size are not correlated in this run.
  - *The motion rate at the outlier is the highest in the run, but that is the stall's own
    consequence, not its cause.* Model-cloud growth per 50 ticks jumps from ~600 (still) to **+59,784**
    at `L16115`→`L16724` (the outlier window) — the **largest per-tick growth of the five condition-5
    windows**, while its *rate* (~2,564 pts/s by the log's own bracketed timing) is **mid-range, not
    the lowest**: two windows are lower (~2,158 and ~1,610) and one is more than double (~5,515). The
    window's own tick rate had collapsed to 2.6/s. The motion signal itself is absent (Finding 3), so
    growth is not evidence of a sweep. Immediately after, growth
    is +16,220 / +22,854 / +38,274 per 50 ticks (`L16724`→`L17300`→`L17905`→`L18455`) at a healthy
    ~8/s — ordinary sustained hand motion.
  - *There is no "unusually fast" event to find.* The `std::bad_cast` and the oversized frames are
    SDK-internal and recur at bring-up (`L73`, `L76`) and teardown (`L25612`) with the scanner
    stationary. Motion is not a plausible trigger for a C++ `std::bad_cast` in the SDK's IMU-stop
    path.
  - *And the log cannot measure motion speed at all.* It carries **no** IMU, pose-value or
    motion-rate channel: the only IMU mentions are the four `Stop imu stream` lines and the
    bring-up/teardown sensor lines, and the perf window's `pose` field is the *duration of the pose
    query* (`p50=0.00 p95=0.01 max=0.01 ms`), not a pose value. So "moved unusually fast" is
    not directly observable in this evidence at all — the model-cloud growth is a proxy, and the
    proxy is confounded by the tick-rate collapse (see above). The hypothesis can be *ruled out as
    the cause* (the trigger is an SDK-internal exception preceded by oversized frames), but it
    cannot be *positively measured* here.

  **What would settle it.** The trigger is *inside* the vendor SDK, so our log cannot resolve it
  further. Two things would: (a) a re-run of the same 6-condition baseline with the scanner moved
  *deliberately fast* (and, in separate conditions, deliberately slow) — if the stall tracks
  motion speed, the hypothesis is revived; if it lands on the same oversized-frame →
  `bad_cast` → restart sequence regardless of speed, it is confirmed as an SDK defect. (b) The SDK's
  own `VideoSensor.cpp:450` size check is the earliest observable symptom: it fires ~2.6 s before
  the restart, so a build that logs the offending frame's size/expected pair with a timestamp would
  let us correlate the oversize with what the scene was doing. Both need the device; neither is
  answerable from this log.

  **What this changes for the board.** Row #112's record framed the 6410 ms outlier as "likely the
  moment-of or near the browser's own connection/render activity" (its own hypothesis) and the
  follow-up as the 4→5 transition (corrected to inside condition 5). The corrected location is right,
  but the *cause* is neither the browser nor the operator's hand: it is the vendor SDK restarting
  its sensor pipeline, which starved the tick of frames for ~6.4 s. A UI-open condition is not
  needed to reproduce it, and neither is fast motion.

  **Caveat, stated plainly:** this is log forensics, not a reproduction. No run was repeated, no
  device was touched, and the raw log was read only. The 6.4 s stall is *attributed* to the pipeline
  restart by temporal adjacency and by the SDK's own frame-wait accounting; the SDK's internal
  reason for the `std::bad_cast` is not in our log and is not inferred here.
